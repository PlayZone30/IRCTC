# Build What Moves India: Complete Hackathon Guidelines

## Step 1: What to Build
You are tasked with redesigning and rebuilding a public service platform. 
*   **Pick from the 10 Listed Platforms:** You can choose from the following 10 official platforms shown in the presentation:
    1.  **IRCTC** (Indian Railway Catering and Tourism Corporation)
    2.  **Income Tax** (e-Filing Portal)
    3.  **CPGRAMS** (Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System)
    4.  **GST** (Goods and Services Tax)
    5.  **EPFO** (Employees' Provident Fund Organisation)
    6.  **MCA** (Ministry of Corporate Affairs Portal)
    7.  **National Cyber Crime** (Reporting Portal)
    8.  **UMANG** (Unified Mobile Application for New-age Governance)
    9.  **Parivahan Sewa** 
    10. **RTI** (Right to Information Online)
*   **Off-List Picks are Allowed:** If you are highly frustrated with a government site not on this list, you can still submit it. However, your chances might be slightly lower if the judges are unfamiliar with how that specific platform works.
*   **Exceptional Work Gets Seen:** Even if you build something off the list, truly outstanding work will still be noticed by the team.

## Step 2: A Complete Proof of Concept
You are expected to build a comprehensive Proof of Concept (POC), not just a design mockup.
*   **Mock the Backend:** Rebuild the platform end-to-end with mock data, a mock backend, and mock accounts. It does not need to handle real-world scale or production traffic.
*   **Make it Web-Accessible:** Host your project anywhere (e.g., ChatGPT Sites, Vercel, your own domain). **It must open in a web browser.** The judges will not download mobile apps; if it doesn't open in a browser, it won't be reviewed.
*   **Provide Instant Logins:** You must include working test credentials so the evaluating team can immediately log in and try the product exactly like an end-user would.
*   **Prioritize the User Experience:** Only the consumer/end-user side is evaluated. The team will assume you built the admin side perfectly. Real implementation would happen later if your project is selected.

## Step 3: Ideas Over Code
*   **Interfaces & Interactions:** Put your energy into the user experience, interface, and interactions, not the heavy backend plumbing.
*   **Ship Bold, Useful Ideas:** Add unique features like a map integration, a tax calculator, or a chatbot-driven flow. The more unique but genuinely useful to the end consumer, the better.
*   **Build for Busy Citizens:** Users are frustrated and don't have time. They want a solution that is faster and significantly simpler than the site they use today.
*   **Skip the Bells & Whistles:** Flashy elements like crazy 3D or Three.js showpieces might look impressive, but if they don't actually help the end-user accomplish their task faster, they aren't valuable. Useful beats flashy.

## Step 4: Team & Email Rules
*   **Two People, Max:** You can go solo or bring exactly **one** partner. This is a strict hard cap.
*   **Both Must Register:** Your partner must also fill out the official registration link.
*   **One Email, Always:** Your registered email address is your sole identity for every form, result, and invite. No exceptions.
*   **Same Email in Both Rounds:** Do not switch emails. If you do, you will lose access, and the small organizing team cannot recover unregistered IDs at a scale of 5,000+ entries.

## Step 5: Four Things to Submit (Round 1)
When submitting your project, you must provide exactly these four things:
1.  **Live Public Link:** A hosted link that opens in a browser.
2.  **2-Minute Demo Video:** Recorded via Loom, OBS, etc. (Teammates can co-present).
    *   *Minute 1:* Demo the product acting as a citizen.
    *   *Minute 2:* Explain how you built it, why you built it that way, and the decisions you made.
3.  **250-Word Summary:** Provide **exactly** 250 words explaining what the project is and why it is better than the current existing solution.
4.  **Partner's Email ID:** You enter theirs; they enter yours. This cross-match proves you are a real team. If you are going solo, leave this field blank.

## Timeline & Key Dates

### Round 1
*   **August 28 | 8:00 PM IST:** Deadline for Round 1 submissions. (Late submissions will not be accepted to keep it fair).
*   **August 28 - September 1 (Judging):** The VM Content team and the OpenAI team will review all submissions. Due to the massive scale (5,000+ entries), the speaker notes that human errors may occur in judging, and participants should not be discouraged if they aren't selected.

### Mentorship Week (Top 250 Selected)
*   **September 1 - September 7:** The Top 250 projects will be added to a WhatsApp group with five expert mentors (veteran engineers with 10+ years of experience from large tech companies, including OpenAI staff). 
*   **Goal:** You will have one week to utilize this free mentorship to tighten up your project, improve your ideas, and prepare for Round 2. 

### Round 2
*   **September 7:** Round 2 submissions are due. You must submit your updated project using the **exact same email IDs** you used in Round 1. 

### The Finale (Top 10 Selected)
*   **September 8 - 12:** The Top 10 projects are revealed.
*   **September 12:** The Bangalore Finale. The Top 10 teams will present their projects live in Bangalore to an audience of founders, creators, mentors, and government officials. The event will be recorded, and the ultimate winners will be announced that day.
